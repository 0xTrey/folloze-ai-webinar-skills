# Account Brief Builder

Help a marketer understand one target account and choose a bounded campaign idea. Keep seller context and target-company research separate.

Read [context-and-evidence.md](references/context-and-evidence.md), then use [output-template.md](references/output-template.md). A Company Brain or CRM connection is helpful, but a seller summary and target URL can start a draft.

Read [tool-map-use.md](references/tool-map-use.md). Use `tool-map.md` to route CRM history, public research, engagement, intent, and storage. Record map version, operation IDs, windows, and actual coverage. An unavailable signal tool remains Missing; a manual scoped export can substitute without implying live connection verification.

## 1. Confirm seller and target

Use the seller's Company Brain for offering, ICP, personas, messaging, product limits, and usable proof. Ask for seller context or the target domain only if missing or ambiguous. Carry account ID and segment ID from the supplied list; do not create a second identity for the same account.

Accept a campaign goal and CTA when supplied. Otherwise propose one clearly labeled next-step hypothesis. If there is a list of accounts, work within the participant's requested set and show per-account coverage.

## 2. Research the account

Read primary company pages relevant to what the target sells, whom it serves, and the problem the seller addresses. Use official announcements or product sources for business changes, preserving event and publication dates. Do not turn a launch into evidence of demand for the seller.

Capture dated source notes and missing coverage. Do not invent revenue, deal size, employee counts, installed technology, budget, or strategy. If browsing is unavailable, use supplied excerpts and mark public research incomplete.

## 3. Add available account context

Read only participant-supplied exports, notes, or authorized connections within the agreed scope. Check existing relationship, customer/open-opportunity state, interactions, account ownership, and overlap when evidence is available. Retain the employer and relationship at the time of historical interactions.

Keep invitations, sent messages, opens, replies, meetings, and product use distinct. No access does not mean no engagement. A zero count applies only to the stated source, window, and complete retrieved coverage.

For intent and technology signals, record exact topic, period, source, and detection limits. Category activity is not verified interest in the seller. A partner listing is not proof of installation. Keep conflicting fit or relationship signals visible.

## 4. Write the brief

Answer the seven questions in the template. Tie the campaign premise to at least one target fact and one supported seller capability. Label the problem, role relevance, and proposed message as hypotheses unless the account stated them.

Recommend one small test, its owner or suggested owner, next action, learning question, and hold conditions. Include usable proof or a precise proof gap. Do not state that the account is ready to buy or outreach-ready because a brief exists.

## 5. Review and hand off

Check identity, source locators, dates, account specificity, product limits, and evidence labels. Show mismatched ICP evidence and unresolved relationship decisions before the recommended play.

Return the brief and source register with review status, coverage gaps, and next action. Pass the same account and segment IDs to Buyer Group Mapper. Keep the private research brief out of buyer-facing creative; only approved, appropriate facts may move into copy. Stop before sending, CRM changes, or campaign builds.
