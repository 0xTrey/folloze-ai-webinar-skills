# The email and LinkedIn framework

## Select the reason to write

Choose the trigger before the wording. Record its source, date and connection to the buyer's work in review notes.

| Route | Use when | Opening |
|---|---|---|
| Personal trigger | A relevant post, promotion, new role or certificate has a verified date within four calendar months | One factual observation connected to the message |
| Company trigger | A sourced launch, event or other company change has a known date and is still relevant | Explain the marketing implication as a hypothesis |
| Company context | Current product, audience or content research is available without an event trigger | A concrete company fact connected to the recipient's function |
| Role or customer story | Suitable personal and company hooks are unavailable | One recognizable work problem or supported customer story |

Calculate the personal-hook cutoff from the drafting date, not the date a profile was retrieved. For October 15, the cutoff is June 15. Unknown or older dates select a fallback. A current headline can establish role relevance without claiming that someone recently started. Former-customer employment requires dated affiliation evidence; it does not prove the person used the product. Do not force congratulations or mention an unrelated personal achievement.

## Five connected sections

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| Subject | Name value supported by the body and offer. Example: Giving Sellers More Insights. Aim for 2-6 words and at most 70 characters; honor stricter destination limits. A personal reference is optional when it earns its place. |
| Opening | Use the selected trigger, company fact, work problem or sourced customer story. Keep the first sentence under 15 words. Do not lead with a generic discovery question or invent customer emotions. |
| Company and role bridge | Explain why that same problem matters to this account and job. Public company research is enough. Keep assumptions conditional. |
| Approved pitch | Insert one exact pitch from the supplied approved library. Preserve its ID, version and approval state. Propose new wording separately. |
| Content CTA | Offer the story, guide or personalized page that answers the opening. The named customer must match the content offer. |

Write near 70-110 body words when practical, with separate short paragraphs and the CTA on its own line. Use one CTA and the supplied signature. Preserve the approved pitch before shortening surrounding copy. Keep evidence and review notes outside the buyer's email. Do not append invented footer copy.

## Function and seniority

Choose function first, then seniority. A seniority label alone is not a message angle.

| Function | Relevant value | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| ABM | Relevant experiences for different people in an account, with reusable campaign foundations | Do not promise an unsupported campaign count or unlimited scale |
| Demand generation | Carry a campaign or intent theme into relevant content, then useful engagement context | Account intent is not evidence of individual interest |
| Field and events | A connected pre-event, event and follow-up content path | Do not imply attendance or an upcoming event without a source |
| Product and content | Turn product explanations and approved assets into useful buyer paths | Do not assume the account has a content-production problem |
| Marketing operations | Brand rules, review, deployment, integrations and engagement handoff | Describe only supported and approved capabilities |

## Choose for the buyer's work

| Role | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| CMO / VP | Business outcome, account relevance and useful context for sales |
| Director / manager | Program consistency, team execution and handoff |
| Practitioner | The task they own, work it removes and an example they can apply |

Choose the function first, then seniority, account evidence, useful content and within-company variety. Example angles:

| Angle | Useful problem | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Product explanation | Different buying roles need different proof | Personalized campaign example |
| Existing content | Approved assets sit unused while campaigns are rebuilt | Small-team customer story |
| Campaign reuse | Audience changes create more builds and reviews | Campaign example |
| Event follow-up | An attendee list does not explain the next useful topic | Event engagement case study |
| Buying groups | One asset rarely answers every account stakeholder | Role-relevant experience |
| Demand conversion | The click's destination leaves the next question unanswered | Campaign example |
| Brand consistency | More teams create more variation in campaign execution | Governed campaign example |
| Engagement context | Visit totals do not show what a buyer found useful | Sales-insight customer story |
| Account scale | Every account needs relevance with finite production capacity | Scale-with-small-team story |
| Seller insight | Sellers lack context for the next conversation | Strategic-account customer story |
| AI deployment | Generated content still needs a live campaign experience | Deployment guide |
| Agent infrastructure | Hosting, brand, integrations and personalization still need management | AI campaign guide |

Use the supplied seller's capabilities and resources. These angles do not establish product claims or permission to reuse a customer story. Supply your own approved pitch and content library.

## LinkedIn formats

Use the sender's supplied voice. Do not inherit another rep's greeting, intentional grammar mistakes or sign-off.

- **Connection note:** a short reason to connect, about 200 characters as a drafting target, subject to the actual channel limit. No full pitch is required. A link is optional; any included link follows the campaign's destination policy.
- **First substantive message:** aim for 45-80 words. Use one relevant opening, a brief role bridge, one exact approved pitch and a useful content CTA. Put the CTA on its own line. Select a shorter approved pitch or flag a length gap if needed.
- **Follow-up:** aim for 45-80 words. Add relevant proof or a complementary resource without repeating the first hook, reintroducing the seller or writing “just checking in.” Keep one CTA. In a board-led campaign, the CTA points to the board or a verified item there.

Return recipient-facing copy without bucket labels, source notes or model commentary inside it. Keep the review rationale in separate fields. Drafting a message never creates a connection request or an automated LinkedIn send.

## Proof and follow-ups

Use the participant's current relationship rules and approved proof library. An old customer roster is a candidate source, not a verified current relationship or permission to name every entry. Route known customers and active opportunities to their appropriate motion before preparing net-new follow-up.

Choose proof by industry, buying problem and use-case relevance. Name recognition is a tiebreaker. One useful example is enough; do not fill a logo quota. A direct competitor can be relevant when its use is permitted. If no supported peer proof exists, use an approved capability example or resource without claiming customer adoption.

Separate these claims: customer relationship, specific product or AI workflow used, and measured outcome. Evidence for one does not establish the others. Do not claim that named customers build campaigns “in seconds,” run “25+ campaigns a month,” or use a named LLM without evidence and approval for that claim. Keep numerical proof at its actual customer, period and scope.

For email follow-up, use a fresh value subject when starting a new thread, or preserve the real thread subject in an actual reply. Do not invent Re: or Fwd:. Use the same shorter 45-80 word target as LinkedIn, with new proof or a complementary resource and one board-led CTA. Do not repeat a personal hook merely to make the message seem individualized.

## Reusable drafting prompts

Use the supplied account, buyer, pitch and content inputs. Replace the braces; a missing personal hook selects the company or role fallback.

**Email first touch**

```text
Using this account brief and buyer role, select a verified relevant trigger or use company context. Personal posts, promotions, new roles and certificates must be dated within four calendar months. Write an opening under 15 words, a company-and-role bridge, one exact approved pitch and a useful content CTA to {campaign board URL}. Aim for 70-110 body words, with a 2-6 word value subject and the CTA on its own line. Keep the supplied signature and footer policy. Return the copy plus separate trigger, source, pitch and link-review fields. Do not send.
```

**LinkedIn first message**

```text
Adapt the selected account angle for a LinkedIn message to {buyer role}. Use a short relevant opening and one exact approved pitch. Aim for 45-80 words and one useful content CTA to {campaign board URL}. Use company context if no suitable personal trigger is supplied. Keep the message free of research labels and commentary; return evidence and unresolved identity/link fields separately. Do not send.
```

**Proof-led follow-up, either channel**

```text
Read the prior message and current relationship rules. Draft a 45-80 word {email or LinkedIn} follow-up with a new proof point or complementary resource. Select proof by relevant use case first, recognition second. Verify permission and the exact customer claim; do not imply a named customer uses an AI workflow from a logo alone. Preserve any approved pitch verbatim. Do not repeat the opening hook or add a generic reminder. Use one content CTA to {campaign board URL}; keep review notes separate. Do not send or schedule.
```

## Three passes

**Plan:** Select channel, touch, trigger route, buyer problem, desired value, framework ID, opening approach, pitch ID, resource ID and source IDs. Record trigger date, drafting date, role hypothesis and missing facts. Choose content before writing the CTA.

**Write:** Return subject, opening, bridge, exact pitch and CTA as separate fields, plus the complete draft. A customer story requires a supported claim. Without one, use a direct problem. Company context may replace unavailable individual research. Account-level intent never becomes an individual's behavior.

**Review:** Check the whole argument. Does the subject promise the value the content provides? Do all five sections address the same problem? Is a personal trigger timely and relevant? Are claims sourced and the pitch unchanged? Does the CTA point to the promised content on the reviewed board? Keep a draft's wording approval separate from sending eligibility. Avoid “caught my eye,” “impressive,” “no small lift,” unrelated praise and invented familiarity.

## Same-company variation

Group sales drafts and supplied prior messages by company, campaign and touch across AgentMail, Smartlead and other sales senders. Require distinct normalized subjects, openings and complete messages in that group. Compare the meaningful copy, excluding greetings and recipient URL parameters.

Reuse approved pitches. Reuse across different companies is allowed. Change a relevant angle, role implication or content offer, rather than names, punctuation or cosmetic synonyms. If relevant options run out, return a review gap. Rich account-level Marketing Emails may share copy and use their own rules.

Compare companion email and LinkedIn drafts for repetitive openings and offers, while keeping the conversation coherent. Do not send the same full message to the same person in two channels. The bundled email checker catches exact normalized repeats. A reviewer still checks semantic similarity, trigger freshness, length targets and LinkedIn copy. Its read-only comparison file is not a persistent reservation or send-attempt ledger. Variation does not establish inbox placement.
